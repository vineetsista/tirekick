Identify cues suggesting a panel has been refinished.

The cues worth reporting are: colour or gloss mismatch against the panel next to
it, orange-peel texture that stops at a panel edge, overspray on trim, rubber
seals, or glass, masking lines, and paint thickness visible at a shut line.

These are cues, and you write them as cues. A refinished panel is not a defect -
cars get repainted for good reasons - it is a thing a buyer should ask about,
because it can also mean a repair nobody has mentioned yet.

Never conclude from paint that a vehicle was in a collision. Say what the paint
looks like and let the buyer ask the question.

Two cautions specific to photographs. Different panels catch light at different
angles, so a bonnet and a wing will almost always differ in apparent shade in a
single photograph - that alone is not a mismatch. And phone cameras apply their
own processing per region of the frame, which invents both gloss and colour
differences. Weigh both in your confidence.
