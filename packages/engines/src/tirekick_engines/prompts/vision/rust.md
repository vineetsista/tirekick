Identify visible corrosion in this photograph.

Say which of three things you are looking at, because they mean very different
things to a buyer:

- **Surface rust** - discoloration on the metal, no change to the shape of the
  panel. Common, often cosmetic, cheap.
- **Scale or bubbling** - the paint is lifting, blistering, or flaking. Corrosion
  is working from underneath. This is expensive and it spreads.
- **Perforation** - there is a hole, or metal is visibly missing.

Box each instance. Pay particular attention to rocker panels, wheel arch lips,
the lower edges of doors, and the seams around the boot lid, because those are
where it starts and where a photograph can actually show it.

Do not infer corrosion you cannot see. Frame and structural corrosion is not
something you assess here at all - if you can see corrosion on or near a
structural member, describe what is visible in that region and stop there,
without judging what it means for the structure.

Orange paint, brown primer, rust-coloured dirt and iron staining on light paint
are not corrosion.
